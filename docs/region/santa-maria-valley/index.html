regionSanta Maria Valley

A funnel opening west to the Pacific at 34.9°N — further south than anywhere sensible for pinot noir, and colder through the growing season than most of Oregon. The transverse ranges turn this valley east-west, the ocean walks straight in, and summer averages 75°F.

Written down by US Alcohol and Tobacco Tax and Trade Bureau (1981)

The story Cited

Grapes grew here in the 1830s, under the Mexican land grants. Modern planting starts in 1964 with a hundred acres; by the mid-1970s there were over 2,000. The AVA came on 5 August 1981 — the country's third, California's second, and the first for either county. In 2010 the TTB pushed the southern line out to the watershed, adding 18,790 acres and nine vineyards.

How it is done Cited

Morning fog takes hours to burn off and afternoon wind replaces it. Winkler Region I. Under 14 inches of rain in a normal year, so nearly everything is irrigated.

Today Cited

Pinot noir and chardonnay are the appellation's two flagship varieties — and the same valley cooks the beef that made it famous, over oak cut off these hills.
