a directory of one grapePinot Country

one thin-skinned grape, the ground it answers to, and what gets eaten beside it.

Thin skin, tight bunches, early ripening. It rots in the rain, burns in the heat, mutates when nobody is looking, and people keep planting it colder and further out anyway — Burgundy, the Ahr, the Willamette, a fog corridor in Sonoma, a valley in Santa Barbara County that runs the wrong way, and a schist basin in Otago at 45° south.
